Allocation rules

  1. Meet own demand. Use own solar, then battery energy above reserve.
  2. Protect essential services. Serve clinic and drinking water first, home essentials second, enterprises third, optional loads last.
  3. Distribute fairly within a tier. Small allocation rounds favour lower essential coverage and past unmet need.
  4. Respect constraints. Available energy, credits, 8 kWh/h building connections and the shared bus limit bound every trade.
  5. Store the remainder. Charge with unused solar; report excess as curtailed energy.

Fairness operates within available supply and credit budgets. It does not guarantee free energy or universal coverage.

What these metrics mean

Unmet energy: demand minus energy actually served.

Critical coverage: fraction of clinic and drinking-water demand served.

Renewable utilisation: 1 − curtailed solar / generated solar. Stored solar counts as utilised.

Essential fairness: Jain’s index across the six homes’ essential-demand coverage fractions. Shown for the latest hour; all-zero coverage gives 0.

A higher fairness score alone does not prove adequate supply. Read it alongside coverage and unmet demand. No grid prices or income estimates are assumed.

Explain the architecture

Inputs → agents → allocation → simulated delivery → local ledger → deferred settlement.

Each building has a software agent with separate resources and decisions. A common scheduler coordinates them inside this browser; this is not a distributed deployment.

Fixed demo price: 2 energy credits per kWh. Credits are accounting units with no monetary redemption.

Time step: 1 hour. A building connection is limited to 8 kWh per step. Battery charge and discharge efficiency: 95% each.

This is a simplified energy-balance model. It does not solve voltage, frequency, phase, AC power flow or electrical protection.
